Scatter plots: a hit within tolerance, and the tolerance ladder

A scatter plot has no x-order to interpolate along, so its metrics pair points and count hits. Scatteract counts a predicted point as a true positive when it lies within 2% of a ground-truth point in range-normalized chart coordinates and reports precision, recall and F1. CHART-Info replaces the fixed circle by a capped, covariance-scaled Mahalanobis distance solved as an assignment problem. Newer benchmarks keep the hit idea but report it at several tolerances at once, because the accuracy of today’s extractors drops by tens of points between ±5% and ±2%.

Figure 4. Left: 24 ground-truth points (blue) with tolerance circles, and their digitizations (orange); green links are hits, red links misses, hollow orange points are spurious. Right: the hit rate as a continuous function of the tolerance, with markers at 1, 2, 5 and 10%; the slope of that curve is the precision cliff.

What to try

  • Leave the noise at its default and drag the tolerance from 10% down to 1%. The hit rate stays near 100% until the circle shrinks to the size of the digitizing noise, then falls off a cliff. The “Drop from 5% to 2%” card is the quantity PlotGround reports for frontier models: 11 to 24 points.
  • Add spurious points. Recall and the hit-rate curve do not move; precision and F1 do. Scatteract’s success rule needs F1 above 0.8, so enough stray points fail the plot even when every true point was found.
  • Switch to one-to-one assignment and raise the noise until two digitized points fall near the same ground-truth point. Nearest-point matching lets both count as hits; the assignment gives the second one to another point or leaves it unmatched.
  • Watch the CHART-Info card while increasing the noise. Its distance is scaled by the spread of the ground-truth cloud and capped so that a point 5% of the mean away already scores zero, so it reaches its floor before the 5% circle does.

How it is computed

Scatteract: predicted point p is a true positive if ‖(p − g)‖ ≤ τ in chart coordinates normalized by the true x- and y-ranges (τ = 2% in the paper) precision = TP ÷ Nᵖʳᵉᵈ, recall = TP ÷ Nᴳᵀ, F1 = harmonic mean; a plot is “successfully extracted” when F1 > 0.8 CHART-Info point-set metric: D(g, p) = min(1, √((g − p)ᵀ V⁻¹ (g − p)) ÷ γ), V = covariance of the ground-truth points, diagonal of V⁻¹ capped at 400 ÷ mean²; cost matrix padded with 1 to K = max(N, M); score = 1 − (assignment cost) ÷ K Tolerance ladder: recall at τ = 1, 2, 5 and 10% of the range (PlotGround and PlotPick report accuracy at such bands; Bar-JEPA uses 5% relaxed and 2% strict)

Notes on this implementation

  • The nearest-point rule follows Scatteract’s text, which pairs each predicted point with its closest true point; two predictions can therefore claim the same true point. The one-to-one mode pads the distance matrix and solves the assignment with the Hungarian algorithm before applying τ.
  • The CHART-Info document does not give the scale γ; γ = 1 here. With the cap on V⁻¹, a point that is 5% of the coordinate mean away in one direction reaches the maximum penalty, as the document notes.
  • Points live in a unit square, so a tolerance of 2% is a circle of radius 0.02; Scatteract normalizes each axis by its own range, which is the same thing here.
  • The 24 ground-truth points are two synthetic clusters; the digitization adds uniform jitter, drops every third point up to the chosen count, and adds spurious points at random positions.
