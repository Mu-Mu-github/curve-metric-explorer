Curve-metric explorer II8 interactive figures, offline

How is a digitized curve scored? The common criteria

Eight interactive figures on the criteria that most published benchmarks share when they score the digitization of a line plot or a scatter plot: one-to-one pairing, error on a common grid, a hit within tolerance, the choice of normalizer, axis calibration, coverage and failure rules, order-aware distances for shifts and loops, and a final scorecard that shows which criterion catches which defect. Move the sliders and the scores are recomputed as you watch. Everything runs offline in your browser.

List of figures

  1. Figure 1Six decisions behind every curve scorePair, sample, measure, normalize, aggregate, and decide what missing data costs: the choices that define each benchmark, side by side.
  2. Figure 2Pairing series one-to-one: recall, precision and swapped labelsSeveral curves in one plot are paired by assignment before anything is scored; the denominator decides whether missing and extra curves cost, and crossing curves can be right in value but wrong in identity.
  3. Figure 3Line plots: error on a shared grid, and what you divide byThe same digitized curve under four normalizers: the value itself, the y-range, the chart maximum and the minor tick interval.
  4. Figure 4Scatter plots: a hit within tolerance, and the tolerance ladderPoints are unordered, so a digitized point is correct if it lands within a tolerance of its partner; the tolerance you choose decides the score, and the PlotGround precision cliff shows why benchmarks now report several.
  5. Figure 5Pixels before values: axis calibrationA curve traced perfectly in the image can still come out wrong in data units; CHART-Info scores the two steps separately, and a log axis multiplies the damage.
  6. Figure 6Coverage and failures: scoring a whole benchmarkTwenty figures at once: how output success, trusted usability and FPC-NRMSE account for empty, truncated and over-counted outputs, and why averaging only over successes misleads.
  7. Figure 7Loops and shifts: order-aware distancesA cyclic voltammogram has two currents at every potential; vertical-error metrics break, and the order-preserving distances (Fréchet, ECS) are what remain.
  8. Figure 8One digitization, every criterionSwitch defects on and off and watch nine scores respond; the table shows which criterion catches which defect, against the disagreement between two human annotators.

The companion collection, the first Curve-metric explorer, walks through individual published metrics (CHART-Info step by step, three coverage rules, pixel matching, Fréchet, DTW versus ECS, RMS versus derived quantities). This second collection is organized by criterion rather than by paper, adds scatter plots, axis calibration, normalizers, whole-benchmark failure accounting and closed loops, and ends with a scorecard that runs every criterion on one digitization at once.

Running the figures

Open index.html in a current version of Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari. There is nothing to install, no network connection is needed, and no data leaves your computer. Each figure is also a complete page on its own, so a single file can be shared or projected. To put the collection online, copy the folder to any static web host.

About these demos

All curves are synthetic, and the metrics are teaching implementations written from the published definitions. Where a choice had to be made that the sources do not fix, it is listed on that figure’s page under “Notes on this implementation”. To benchmark real digitizations, use the official scorers, such as the CHART-Info metric document, the ExChart and EpiCurveBench code, and the DigitizerBench definitions.
