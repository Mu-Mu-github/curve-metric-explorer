Line plots: error on a shared grid, and what you divide by

For a line plot every common metric does the same two things first: it reads both curves at the same x positions, after linear interpolation, and takes the vertical difference there. The metrics part company at the next step, the normalizer. CHART-Info and RMS divide by the value itself, FPC-NRMSE and ECS by the y-range, Adaptive MAPE by the chart maximum, and Tick-Based Error by the minor tick interval. Apply a constant vertical offset and watch which of them treats the flat start of the curve differently from its top.

Figure 3. A polarization-like curve rising from 0.02 to 8 mA cm⁻² (blue) and its digitization (orange). Red bars mark the vertical difference at every tenth grid position. The strip below shows the per-position error under the selected normalizer, capped at 1; the cards give the aggregates of all four at once.

What to try

  • Keep the default offset of +0.1 and switch between the four normalizers. Dividing by the value saturates at the cap along the whole flat start, where the current is only 0.02: a constant miss of 0.1 is 500% of the value there and 1% of the value at the top. The three other normalizers show a flat strip, because the miss is the same everywhere.
  • Set the offset to 0 and the x-shift to +0.03. Now every normalizer concentrates the error on the steep part of the curve, where a small horizontal shift becomes a large vertical one. Vertical-difference metrics cannot tell a shift from a value error; Figure 7 shows the alternative.
  • Change the major tick spacing from 2 to 4. Nothing in the curves changes, yet the TBE card and the “within one minor tick” card both improve: the tick-based tolerance follows the chart’s own granularity, which is its purpose and also its caveat.
  • Raise the click noise with the offset at 0. The “within 5% of the value” card collapses first at the low end, where 5% of 0.02 is a few thousandths; the “within one tick” card is indifferent to where the noise sits.

How it is computed

Both curves are read at K = 610 positions, one per horizontal plot pixel (FPC-NRMSE convention), by linear interpolation; Δₖ = ŷₖ − yₖ. CHART-Info recall: Σᵢ (1 − min(1, |Δ(uᵢ)| ÷ |y(uᵢ)|)) · Intervalᵢ ÷ (uₘ − u₁) at the 17 ground-truth points uᵢ NRMSE: √(mean Δₖ²) ÷ (yₘₐₓ − yₘᵢₙ) (the e of FPC-NRMSE) Adaptive MAPE: 100 · mean |Δₖ| ÷ max|y|, each term capped at 100% Tick-Based Error: mean min(1, |Δₖ| ÷ t), t = major tick interval ÷ 5 Hit rates: fraction of positions with |Δₖ| ≤ 0.05 |yₖ|, and with |Δₖ| ≤ t

Notes on this implementation

  • The ground truth is a synthetic sigmoid from 0.02 to 8; the digitized curve is the same function shifted, offset and jittered, clipped at zero. Values are read on a pixel-column grid over the full range, so coverage is complete here; Figure 6 treats partial coverage.
  • Adaptive MAPE is defined for table cells and divides by the largest absolute ground-truth value of the chart; applied to a curve sampled on a grid, as here, it is the range-normalized MAE whenever the minimum is near zero. ExChart’s code normalizes scatter x and y separately.
  • Tick-Based Error is defined inside the RMS table matching; its estimate t = major interval ÷ 5 is used here directly on grid positions.
  • The hit-rate cards use positions on the grid, not points, so they weight every stretch of x equally.
