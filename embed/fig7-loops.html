Loops and shifts: order-aware distances

Every metric in Figures 2, 3 and 5 reads one curve at the other’s x positions. That assumes y is a single-valued function of x. A cyclic voltammogram, a Nyquist plot or a hysteresis loop is not: the sweep goes out and comes back. Sorting such points by x and interpolating produces a curve that never existed. The remaining options keep the order of the points along the sweep: the discrete Fréchet distance (the longest leash of an order-preserving walk, used by MatViX), the gap-penalized alignment of ECS, and a tolerance band drawn along the curve.

Figure 7. A synthetic voltammogram: the forward sweep carries an oxidation peak, the return sweep a reduction peak. The digitization (orange) can be shifted along the potential axis, jittered and cut short. The side panel shows what the selected method computes: the leash length along the walk, the alignment cost matrix, or the band test.

What to try

  • Stay on vertical error after sorting by x. The red zigzag is what an x-grid metric actually compares: at each potential the forward and return currents are interleaved, and the “curve” jumps between the two branches. The NRMSE card is a number, but not a measure of this digitization.
  • Switch to the Fréchet view and set the shift to 0 and the noise to 0. The distance is close to zero. Now add a potential shift: the longest leash sits on the steep flank of a peak, and its length is the geometric distance between the two loops, not the vertical gap.
  • Cut the loop short with Fraction traced at 60%. Fréchet must still reach the end of the ground truth, so the last leash spans the missing return sweep; ECS instead charges one gap per missing point, and the band recall reports exactly the share of the loop that was traced.
  • In the ECS view, raise θ from 3% to 10%. Pairs that were “fully wrong” become partial credits and the alignment straightens. EpiCurveBench’s default of 1% is meant for counts read off a chart, which is much stricter than typical digitizing noise on a loop.

How it is computed

Coordinates are normalized to the unit square (potential 0–1, current −0.8–1.0) before any distance is taken. Discrete Fréchet: dₔ(G, P) = min over couplings of max ‖gᵢ − pⱼ‖, couplings starting at (1, 1), ending at (n, m), advancing i, j or both ECS (ERP) on the ordered points: pair cost = ‖p − t‖ if ≤ θ, else 1; gap cost λ = 1; ECS = 1 − C(M, N) ÷ (matches + gaps) Band recall = share of ground-truth points within δ of the digitized polyline; band precision = share of digitized points within δ of the ground-truth polyline Vertical NRMSE after sorting: both point lists sorted by x, linearly interpolated on 200 positions of the shared x-range, RMSE ÷ current range

Notes on this implementation

  • ECS is defined for one-dimensional series with the error divided by the y-range; here the pair cost is a two-dimensional Euclidean distance in normalized coordinates, which is the natural extension for a loop and this demo’s choice.
  • Arc-length parameterization is implicit: both loops are sampled at equal steps of the sweep parameter, so the point index plays the role of arc length. A digitizer that returns points in a different order (for example sorted by x) would have to be re-ordered along the curve first.
  • The discrete Fréchet distance has a sampling floor set by the point spacing (72 points per loop here); resample densely before comparing digitizations of different density.
  • The loop is synthetic: a forward sweep with a Gaussian oxidation peak and a return sweep with a smaller reduction peak, offset by a capacitive current.
