One digitization, every criterion

No single number catches every way a digitization can be wrong. Here one figure with two series is scored by all the criteria of the previous pages at once. Each checkbox injects one defect; the table compares every score with its value for a clean digitization and marks the criteria that move. The last row is the noise floor: the same scores for two careful human annotators of the same figure.

Figure 8. Left: two ground-truth series (blue) and their digitizations (orange) with the chosen defects. Right: each criterion’s score for the clean digitization, for the current one, and whether the change exceeds the two-annotator noise floor for that criterion.

What to try

  • Leave only x-shift on. The vertical-error family (6b, NRMSE, tick error, RMS) all move, concentrated on the flank of the peak; the coverage and the derived peak height do not. The Fréchet distance moves by about the shift itself, which is the honest size of the error.
  • Switch to calibration scale ×1.08 alone. Everything that compares values moves by about 8%; the derived peak position passes while peak height fails; the swap diagnostic and coverage stay flat. Compare with Figure 5: pixel-space scores would not see this at all.
  • Truncated at x = 0.75: coverage and FPC-NRMSE collapse to failure and ECS pays a gap per missing point, while NRMSE on the overlap barely notices. The derived value at x = 0.8 cannot be read at all.
  • Stray line: only the criteria with max(N, M) in the denominator (6b, FPC via the line count) react; per-series errors are unchanged. Labels swapped: the swap diagnostic jumps while the values-only view is unchanged. One stray click: the Fréchet distance, the maximum, reacts far more than any mean.
  • Tick every box. No single row is red for every defect, and no defect is caught by every row. That is the argument for reporting a small set of complementary numbers rather than one.

How it is computed

Every score is computed as on its own page: series paired by assignment on 1 − NRMSE; CHART-Info 6b with ε = range ÷ 100 and linear extrapolation; NRMSE, Adaptive MAPE (÷ 0.85, the chart maximum) and tick error on the shared 61-point grid over the overlap; FPC image score = mean NRMSE if two series were found with coverage ≥ 0.8, else 1; RMS-F1 on cells keyed “series|x” at the eleven x tick labels, τ = 0.5, θ = 0.1, Hungarian matching; discrete Fréchet on the 61 points of each matched pair, maximum over pairs; ECS on the y sequences with θ = 0.01, λ = 1, averaged; derived quantities: peak position of G1 within ±0.01, peak height within ±5%, value of G1 at x = 0.8 within ±5%; swap diagnostic = values-only similarity − label-aware similarity (Figure 2).

Notes on this implementation

  • The noise-floor column is this demo’s construction: two synthetic annotators who add ±0.004 of independent click noise and nothing else. On real figures the floor must be measured by digitizing the same figures twice.
  • “Catches it” is a threshold of twice the noise floor (or 0.005 where the floor is zero); it is a reading aid, not a published rule.
  • The chart maximum used by Adaptive MAPE is the peak of G1 (about 0.85); the tick interval t = 0.04 is one fifth of the 0.2 major spacing shown on the axis.
  • Series are synthetic: a peak on a rising baseline and a falling straight line that cross near x = 0.85; the stray line is a flat line at 0.5 with no legend label.
