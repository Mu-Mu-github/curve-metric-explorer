Coverage and failures: scoring a whole benchmark

A benchmark is many figures, and some digitizations fail outright: nothing returned, a curve that stops early, a gridline counted as a series. If the error is averaged only over the outputs that exist, a tool that gives up on hard figures looks accurate. DigitizerBench’s three numbers fix this: output success counts outputs, trusted usability counts outputs that pass a structural gate, and FPC-NRMSE gives every untrusted figure the worst score before averaging. PlotExtract instead reports the missed range beside the overlap error. Drag the sliders to change how many of the twenty figures fail in each way.

Figure 6. Each tile is one figure: ground truth in blue, digitization in orange, the coverage bar beneath, and the figure’s FPC score in the corner. Green frames pass the trust gate, amber frames fail it (short coverage or wrong line count), grey tiles returned nothing. The bar below the tiles shows the shares that the three DigitizerBench numbers count.

What to try

  • Start from the defaults and read the cards: the error over outputs only is a few hundredths, while FPC-NRMSE is about 0.4. The difference is entirely the eight figures that failed the gate and were charged the maximum. This is the survivorship bias the metric was built to remove.
  • Raise the coverage of truncated figures to 80%. They cross the gate, trusted usability jumps, and FPC-NRMSE drops even though not a single value changed: the gate is a step, and the 80% threshold is a design choice of the paper, not a physical constant.
  • Move all figures to “no output” except a few. Output success falls, trusted usability falls with it, and the identity card shows that FPC-NRMSE is now almost entirely a failure rate. In DigitizerBench’s own table, LinePilot (OCR) at 0.672 owes about 0.62 of that to untrusted images and about 0.05 to error.
  • Set the typical error high. The identity still holds, and when the error of a trusted figure reaches 1 it is capped: no single figure can weigh more than a failure.

How it is computed

Per figure i: eᵢ = RMSE ÷ y-range of its matched line(s); Cᵢ = covered share of the plot width Trust gate Tᵢ = 1 iff the line count is correct, every match has finite error, at least 20 valid points, and Cᵢ ≥ 0.80 Per-figure score Fᵢ = eᵢ if Tᵢ = 1 and eᵢ < 1, otherwise 1 FPC-NRMSE = mean Fᵢ OS = share of figures with non-empty output TU = share with Tᵢ = 1 Because every untrusted figure scores exactly 1: FPC-NRMSE = (1 − TU) + TU · ēₜᵣᵤₛₜₑₔ PlotExtract (per figure with output): MAE on the overlapping x-range, and the missed x-range at the two ends, reported separately

Notes on this implementation

  • Twenty synthetic figures share one ground-truth curve; each digitization carries a random error level drawn around the chosen typical value. A figure with an extra line is given the right number of points and full coverage so that only the line count fails its gate.
  • DigitizerBench pairs lines with the Hungarian algorithm on min(e, 1) + (1 − C) before testing the gate; with one true line per figure the pairing is trivial here, so an extra line can only break the count condition.
  • The 20-point minimum of the gate is assumed satisfied by every output; the paper does not say whether the y-range in eᵢ is the axis range or the data range, and the data range is used here.
  • The published numbers quoted in “What to try” are from Table 3 of the LinePilot paper: FPC-NRMSE 0.672, output success 55.1%, trusted usability 38.2% for LinePilot (OCR) on DigitizerBench-Full.
